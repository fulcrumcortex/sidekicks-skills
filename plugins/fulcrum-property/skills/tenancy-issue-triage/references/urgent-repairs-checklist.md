# Urgent repairs checklist

Every state and territory defines urgent repairs in its residential tenancy law, and the lists, response times and the amount a tenant may spend on an emergency tradesperson differ. Use this checklist to sort the queue, then confirm against the current rules for the property's state before telling anyone what they are entitled to.

## Treat as urgent (same day) until a person confirms otherwise

- [ ] Burst water service or a serious water leak
- [ ] Blocked or broken toilet where there is no other working toilet
- [ ] Serious roof leak
- [ ] Gas leak or smell of gas (tell the tenant to call the gas distributor's emergency line and leave the property if advised; do not wait for a trade)
- [ ] Dangerous electrical fault: exposed wiring, sparking, burning smell, repeated tripping with no obvious cause
- [ ] Flooding, or serious flood, storm or fire damage
- [ ] Failure or breakdown of gas, electricity or water supply to the property
- [ ] Failure of an essential service or appliance for hot water, cooking, heating, cooling or laundry, where the state includes it
- [ ] Any fault or damage that makes the property unsafe or not secure: broken external lock, smashed window, damaged entry door
- [ ] A fault likely to cause injury or serious damage to the property if left
- [ ] Serious fault in a lift or staircase
- [ ] Smoke alarm missing, not working or beeping (states set specific obligations and timeframes)
- [ ] Pool barrier or gate fault where there is a pool

If there is immediate danger to life (fire, gas, live electrical, structural collapse), the first reply tells the tenant to call 000 and the relevant emergency service. The skill does not wait for approval to show that safety advice to the PM; the PM sends it.

## Routine (business-hours response)

- Dripping tap, minor leak contained to a fixture
- One of several appliances not working where an alternative exists
- Cosmetic damage, sticking doors, worn carpet
- Garden, fence and gutter maintenance without safety risk
- Pest sightings without health risk

## Questions to ask the tenant (one message, only what is missing)

1. What exactly is happening, and since when?
2. Is anyone at risk, or is the property not secure?
3. Can the water, gas or power be turned off at the main safely?
4. Photos or a short video
5. Access: who will be home, pets, preferred times, and permission to use the office key

## Owner approval rule of thumb

- Urgent repair: act within the management agreement's emergency authority, then tell the owner the same day.
- Routine repair under the owner's approval limit: issue the work order, then inform the owner.
- Routine repair over the limit or with options: get one to three quotes and request approval before booking.

## Confirm for the property's state

- [ ] The current urgent repairs list
- [ ] Required response time
- [ ] Amount a tenant may spend on an urgent repair if the agent or owner cannot be reached, and how they are reimbursed
- [ ] Smoke alarm and minimum standards obligations

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
