# Candidate privacy and consent

Recruitment agencies hold a lot of personal information about people who are not their clients. The Privacy Act and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) apply to most agencies (and to small businesses that trade in personal information). Use these rules unless the agency's own privacy policy is stricter.

## Collection notice (APP 5)

At or before first collection, or as soon as practicable after, tell the candidate:

- who the agency is and how to contact it;
- why their information is collected (to find them suitable roles and represent them to clients);
- who it may be disclosed to (prospective employers, referees, background or reference check providers), and whether any are overseas;
- what happens if they do not provide it;
- that the privacy policy explains access, correction and complaints, with a link.

Short notice for a first message:

> {agency_name} collects your details to consider you for roles and, with your permission each time, to put you forward to employers. We won't send your details to any employer without asking you first. Our privacy policy explains how to access or correct your information or make a complaint: {privacy_policy_link}.

## Collect only what is needed (APP 3)

- Collect what the role and the process need: contact details, work history, qualifications, work rights, availability, salary expectations.
- Sensitive information (health, criminal record, racial or ethnic origin, religion, union membership, sexual orientation and similar) is collected only with consent and only when reasonably necessary for the role, for example a criminal history check required by law for the job.
- Do not record protected attributes volunteered in a screening call unless the candidate asks for an adjustment; then record only the adjustment needed.

## Consent log

Keep a consent record for each candidate, per action:

| Consent | When needed | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| To be represented for a specific role and client | Before sending any identifying details to that client | Role, client name, date and time, channel, the words used |
| To contact each referee | Before the reference call | Referee names the candidate supplied, date |
| To contact a current employer | Explicit and separate | Never assume |
| For background checks (police check, qualification check) | Before the check | Provider, check type, signed consent form |
| To receive job alerts or marketing | Before sending | Spam Act consent and unsubscribe status |

Consent for one client and role does not cover another. Consent given six months ago for a different role does not cover today's submission.

## Sending candidate details to a client

- Send only what the client needs at that stage: a de-identified summary first if the agency works that way, the full CV only after consent and interest.
- Remove photos, dates of birth, home addresses and referee details from client copies.
- Check that no other agency has already represented the candidate to this client for this role (ask the candidate).

## Retention and disposal (APP 11)

- Keep candidate records only as long as the agency's retention policy says and the law requires. Set a review date on each record.
- At the review date: ask the candidate whether they want to stay on file; if not, or if no response after a reasonable period, destroy or de-identify the record unless the law requires it to be kept.
- The agent flags records past their review date for a person to decide. It never deletes records on its own.

## Access and correction (APP 12 and 13)

A candidate can ask to see or correct their information. Route requests to the agency's privacy contact the same day.

## Security

- No CVs or ID documents in group chats or personal email.
- Share files with clients through links with an expiry where possible, and revoke them when the role closes.
- Report a suspected data breach to the agency's privacy contact immediately; notifiable data breach assessment is their decision.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
