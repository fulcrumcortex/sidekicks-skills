# The advice boundary in renewal work

The user holds, or works under, an Australian financial services licence (AFSL). Their licensee decides what advice is given and by whom. This skill drafts administrative, factual messages and records information. It does not give financial product advice, and it never stands in for the licensee's authorised people.

## What the skill may do

- State facts: the policy number, the insurer, the expiry date, the renewal premium as quoted by the insurer, what documents are needed, how to give instructions.
- Ask factual questions about changes in circumstances (the needs-change prompts).
- Send the client the insurer's or licensee's documents: renewal notice, schedule, Product Disclosure Statement, Target Market Determination link, Financial Services Guide.
- Record the client's instructions and route questions to the authorised person.

## What the skill must not do

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "You should increase your contents cover to $X" | Personal advice: it considers the client's circumstances | "Your current contents sum insured is $X. If the value of your belongings has changed, let us know and {adviser_name} will go through it with you." |
| "This policy is right for you" or "you're well covered" | Personal advice, and possibly misleading | "Here is your renewal schedule showing the cover and sums insured." |
| "Most clients like you add business interruption" | General advice at least; needs the licensee's general advice warning and can drift into personal advice | Leave it out. Let the authorised person discuss options. |
| Comparing insurers or recommending a switch | Advice on a financial product | Route to the authorised person |
| Saying a claim will or will not be paid | Only the insurer decides | "The insurer decides claims under the policy wording. {adviser_name} can talk you through the wording." |
| Telling the client they are covered after expiry | Cover depends on the insurer's records | Confirm with the insurer first |

General advice is advice that does not consider the person's objectives, financial situation or needs; it requires a general advice warning in the licensee's approved form. Personal advice considers, or a reasonable person might expect it to consider, one or more of those things, and carries stronger obligations. If a message could be read either way, it is not sent until the authorised person approves the wording.

## Routing questions

Any of these in a reply or on a call goes to the authorised person the same day, with the thread attached:

- "Should I...?", "Do I need...?", "Is it worth...?", "What would you recommend?"
- Questions about whether something is covered, or about excess and limit choices.
- A claim, a potential claim, or a loss since the last renewal.
- Financial hardship or difficulty paying the premium.
- Signs of vulnerability (confusion, bereavement, serious illness, family violence).

Reply while routing:

> Thanks {first_name}, that's a good question for {adviser_name}, who will call you by {day}. Nothing changes on your policy until you've spoken.

## Disclosure duties at renewal (for the needs-change prompts)

- For consumer insurance (home, contents, motor, travel and similar personal cover), the client has a duty to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation.
- For business and other non-consumer insurance, the duty of disclosure applies at renewal: the client must tell the insurer everything they know, or a reasonable person would know, is relevant to the insurer's decision.

The prompts ask about changes so the client can meet these duties. Use the licensee's or insurer's approved wording to explain the duty; do not paraphrase it.

## Cross-selling and anti-hawking

Contacting an existing client about renewing their policy is expected. Offering a different or additional financial product to a retail client in the course of, or because of, unsolicited contact can breach the anti-hawking prohibition in the Corporations Act. Do not add other products to renewal messages or call scripts. If the client raises a new need, record it and route it to the authorised person, who handles it under the licensee's process.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
