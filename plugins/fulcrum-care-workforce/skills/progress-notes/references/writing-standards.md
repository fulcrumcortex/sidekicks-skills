# Writing standards for progress notes

Read this while drafting. The aim is a note that the participant could read without feeling judged, that another worker could act on, and that would stand up if read by an auditor or a court.

## Rules

1. **Write what happened, not what it meant**, unless the author is giving a professional assessment, which goes in its own labelled section.
2. **Attribute every reported statement.** "Participant reported...", "Mother said...". Never turn a report into a fact.
3. **Be specific.** Times, counts, distances, duration, the prompt level used, the tool or measure named.
4. **Person-first, respectful language.** The person comes before the disability or the behaviour.
5. **Use the participant's own words** for goals, preferences and feelings where the source gives them.
6. **Name others by role**, not by name, and keep their personal details out.
7. **No invented content.** No stock phrases ("engaged well", "had a good day") unless the source says so.
8. **Write promptly and mark late entries.** Never back-date. Corrections are dated addenda, not silent edits.

## Swap list

| Avoid | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| Client was aggressive | Participant raised their voice and pushed the chair back after being asked to stop the game |
| Non-compliant with exercises | Participant completed 2 of 5 home exercises; said "the band hurts my wrist" |
| Attention-seeking | Participant called out 6 times in 10 minutes while the worker was on the phone |
| Refused to engage | Participant said they did not want to do the activity today; offered two alternatives, declined both |
| Wheelchair-bound | Uses a wheelchair |
| Suffers from autism | Autistic person, or person with autism (use the participant's stated preference) |
| Seemed depressed | Participant said "I've been feeling flat all week"; spoke little and kept eyes down for most of the session |
| Had a meltdown | Participant cried and covered their ears for about 5 minutes after the fire alarm test; calmed with headphones |
| Was lazy | Participant stayed in bed until 11:00 and said they were tired |
| Mum is difficult | Mother asked three times for the session to be moved; agreed to Thursday |

## Separating the buckets: worked example

Source voice note (fictional):

> "Saw P-0142 at home, about two to three, mum was there. She was pretty flat, said the new meds make her sleepy. We did the shopping list task, she got through it with me reading the first two items, then she did the rest. Wrote a list of six. I think she's getting more confident. Mum says she fell in the bathroom on Sunday but she's fine. Next week we'll try the shop."

Draft note (goal-linked, extract):

- Admin: start and end `[not stated: "about two to three"]`; author to confirm exact times.
- **Reported:** Participant said her new medication makes her sleepy. Mother reported the participant fell in the bathroom on Sunday and was not hurt.
- **Observed:** Participant wrote a shopping list of six items. Worker read the first two items aloud; participant completed the remaining four without prompting.
- **Interpretation (author):** Author considers the participant's confidence with the task is increasing.
- **Plan:** Next session: practise using the list at a local shop.
- **Incident flag:** A fall was reported. Follow the incident management process and check whether it needs recording or reporting.
- **Needs confirmation:** exact start and end times; whether "flat" was the participant's word or the author's impression (left out of the note until confirmed); whether the medication change is known to the participant's GP (do not add advice to the note).

Notice what was not added: no "had a good session", no reason for the fall, no comment on the medication.
