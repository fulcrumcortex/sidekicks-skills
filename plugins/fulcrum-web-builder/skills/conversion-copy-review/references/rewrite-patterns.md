# Rewrite patterns

## Hero headline patterns

Pick the one that fits the offer; do not combine them.

| Pattern | Shape | Fictional example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome + timeframe | [Result] in/by [time] | "Your bathroom retiled in five working days" |
| Outcome without the pain | [Result] without [hated part] | "Quarterly BAS done without the shoebox of receipts" |
| Audience + outcome | [Result] for [specific audience] | "Rosters that respect award rules, for aged-care providers" |
| Replace the old way | [Old way] replaced by [new way] | "Phone tag replaced by a booking link that fills itself" |
| The number | [Specific number] + [meaning] | "4 hours from enquiry to fixed quote" |

Tests a headline must pass:
- Could a competitor use it unchanged? If yes, it is not specific enough.
- Would the reader say it aloud to a colleague? If not, it is too abstract.
- Does it promise something the page then proves? If not, cut or add proof.
- About 6 to 12 words. Longer is acceptable only when every word adds information.

## Subheadline

One sentence: how it works and for whom, naming the mechanism. "A senior physio sees you the same day, and your health fund rebate is claimed on the spot."

## Calls to action

| Situation | Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demo | Get started | Book a 20-minute walkthrough |
| Quote | Submit | Get my fixed quote |
| Trial | Sign up | Start my 14-day trial (if the trial is real and the length is current) |
| Download | Download | Send me the checklist |
| Call-back | Contact us | Call me back today |
| Low-commitment secondary | Learn more | See a sample report |

Rules:
- First person ("my") often reads naturally on buttons; test it rather than assume.
- Add a line under the button that removes the biggest fear: "No card needed", "We reply within one business day", "Takes about 2 minutes". Only if true.
- One primary button per view. If two actions matter equally, the page is serving two audiences; split it.

## Objection handling

Collect the top objections from sales calls, reviews and support tickets. Typical ones and where to answer them:

| Objection | Answer near | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Too expensive" | Price or offer | Show what it replaces or saves, with the reader's numbers; link pricing |
| "Will it work for me?" | Hero proof, case study | A result from someone like them (industry, size, location) |
| "Too much effort to switch" | How it works | Steps with time per step; who does the work |
| "What if it goes wrong?" | Button | Cancellation terms, guarantee wording approved by the owner |
| "Who are you?" | Footer, about section | Real people, location, ABN, licences, years trading |

## Proof placement

- Above the fold: one item, specific and attributed (rating with count and source, client number, a licence).
- After each major claim: the evidence for that claim, not a generic testimonial.
- Near the final CTA: the strongest single testimonial.
- Format: quote that names a result, then name, role or suburb, and organisation if permitted. "We stopped losing Friday jobs to voicemail" beats "Great service, highly recommend".

## Feature to benefit

| Feature (what it is) | Benefit (what the reader gets) | Proof (why believe it) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automatic SMS reminders | Fewer no-shows | "No-shows fell from 1 in 8 to 1 in 20 at a fictional example clinic over 3 months" |

Lead with the benefit, follow with the feature as the mechanism, then the proof.

## Microcopy

- Form labels say what is needed: "Mobile (we text your booking confirmation)".
- Error messages say how to fix: "Enter a postcode with 4 digits".
- Empty states say what to do next.
- Success messages say what happens next and when.

## Reading level and rhythm

- One idea per sentence. Average under about 15 words for consumer copy; no sentence over about 25.
- Prefer common words: "use" not "utilise", "help" not "facilitate", "about" not "approximately".
- Front-load paragraphs: the first sentence carries the point; the rest supports it.
- Use numerals for numbers ("3 steps", "15 minutes"); they are scannable.
- Australian spelling and terms (organise, colour, enrol, mobile, postcode, GST).

## Worked example (fictional)

Before:

> **Elevate Your Business With Seamless, Cutting-Edge Bookkeeping Solutions**
> We're passionate about empowering small businesses with tailored, best-in-class financial solutions. Get started today!
> [Submit]

After:

> **Your books reconciled every Friday, BAS lodged on time**
> A registered BAS agent reconciles your accounts weekly and lodges each quarter, for sole traders and businesses with up to 20 staff.
> [Book a 15-minute fit call]
> No lock-in contract. We reply within one business day.

Changes: outcome and cadence replace adjectives; the audience is named; "registered BAS agent" is a checkable credential (must be true); the CTA states the action and its size; the line under the button answers the commitment objection. "Best-in-class" was removed because no evidence exists.
