# Waste allowances, units and rounding

## Waste allowance starting points

These are common estimating starting points, not rules. Use the business's own figures whenever they exist, and say on the quote calculation which figures came from the user and which are defaults. Raise the allowance for complex cut-ups, small rooms, patterned layouts and inexperienced crews; lower it for repetitive, standard-module work.

| Category | Starting point | Raise it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timber framing | 10% | Many openings, raked or angled walls |
| Plasterboard | 10–15% | Many openings, short walls, raked ceilings |
| Wall and floor tiles, straight lay | 10% | Small rooms, many cuts, large-format tiles |
| Tiles, diagonal or herringbone | 15% | Always use the higher figure |
| Floor sheeting | 8% | Irregular floor plan |
| Roof sheeting | 5% | Hips, valleys, multiple pitches |
| Insulation batts | 5% | Irregular framing spacing |
| Paint | 10% | Porous or new surfaces needing extra coats (count coats separately) |
| Concrete | 5% | Uneven subgrade, pump waste, small pours |
| Bricks and blocks | 5% | Many corners, openings or face-brick selection |
| Electrical cable | 10% | Long runs with many drops |
| Pipe | 10% | Many fittings and offsets |

Do not apply waste to: items bought as single units (a hot water unit, a door), allowances (PS and PC), or labour.

## Unit conversion

A takeoff measures in the unit of the drawing (m, m2, m3, each). Suppliers sell in purchase units (sheet, length, box, bag, roll, tonne). Convert with a **coverage** figure: how many takeoff units one purchase unit covers.

| Item | Takeoff unit | Purchase unit | Coverage example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plasterboard 2400 x 1200 | m2 | sheet | 2.88 m2 per sheet |
| Plasterboard 3600 x 1200 | m2 | sheet | 4.32 m2 per sheet |
| Tiles 300 x 600, 8 per box | m2 | box | 1.44 m2 per box |
| Framing timber in 2.4 m lengths | lm | length | 2.4 lm per length |
| Paint, 15 L at 16 m2/L, 2 coats | m2 | can | 120 m2 per can |
| Concrete | m3 | m3 | 1 |

Always confirm coverage from the supplier data or the pack. A guessed coverage is as bad as a guessed price; flag it.

## Rounding rules

1. Apply waste to the takeoff quantity.
2. Divide by coverage and round **up** to a whole purchase unit.
3. Round up again to the pack size or minimum order (for example studs sold in packs of 10).
4. For stock shortfall, round the shortfall up to the pack size, not the total.
5. Round money to the cent at the line level, then total. Show GST once, on the subtotal.

## Units that commonly go wrong

- Linear metres versus lengths: 64 studs is not 64 lm.
- Area of walls taken off one side only when both sides are lined.
- Concrete in m2 (slab area) instead of m3 (area x thickness).
- Tiles measured net of openings while the supplier quotes per box including spacer allowance.
- Paint quoted per litre while the takeoff is in m2 with no spread rate or coat count.
