# Quote structure, allowances and checks

## Section order

1. Business details: legal name, ABN, licence number where the trade or state requires one, contact.
2. Customer and site address, quote number, date, validity date, revision number.
3. Scope summary in plain language, referencing the drawings by number and revision.
4. Priced sections (by stage or trade): materials and labour may be combined per section for the customer; keep the detailed build-up internal unless the business shows it.
5. Provisional sums, listed separately with the allowance amount.
6. Prime cost items, listed separately with the allowance amount and what it covers (supply only, or supply and install).
7. Exclusions.
8. Assumptions and site conditions.
9. Totals: subtotal ex GST, GST, total inc GST.
10. Validity, payment terms and acceptance method, using the business's own terms.

## Provisional sums and prime cost items

| | Provisional sum (PS) | Prime cost item (PC) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | An allowance for **work** whose extent cannot be known yet | An allowance for **an item** not yet selected |
| Example | Rock excavation, termite treatment extent, making good after removal of an unknown wall lining | Tapware, appliances, light fittings, tiles at an allowance per m2 |
| What the customer pays | The actual cost of the work, plus the builder's margin if the contract says so | The actual price of the selected item, plus margin if the contract says so |
| Quote must say | What the allowance covers and how adjustments are charged | Whether it is supply only or supply and install, and the allowance per unit |

Rules:

- Never fill a PS or PC with a guessed figure. If the user has no amount, leave it as MISSING and ask.
- Do not hide uncertain work in a lump sum. If its extent depends on what is found on site, it is a PS.
- Many states require allowances to be reasonable estimates and explained in a residential contract. Flag any allowance that looks low compared with the business's own past jobs, and say why.

## Exclusions and assumptions library

Pick only what applies. Each exclusion should be specific enough that the customer understands what they need to arrange.

- Council, certifier and authority fees, unless listed.
- Engineering, surveying and soil testing.
- Asbestos identification and removal. If the building predates 1990, recommend an inspection before demolition.
- Rock or unexpected ground conditions (or include a PS).
- Relocation of services found during work.
- Painting and making good beyond the work area.
- Floor coverings, window furnishings, landscaping.
- Rubbish removal beyond the stated number of skips.
- Work outside normal hours.
- Assumed: clear access for vehicles and materials, power and water on site, the site is free of other trades during the work.

## Validity, GST and deposits

- Default validity: the business's own period. If none is given, suggest 30 days and say so. Shorten it when a supplier price expires before the quote does (the script flags this).
- For volatile materials (steel, copper, timber in a rising market), state which prices are fixed and which are subject to change, and include a rise-and-fall clause only if the business uses one.
- GST: catalogue prices are ex GST; show subtotal ex GST, GST at 10% and total inc GST. If the business is not registered for GST, it must not charge GST; ask before assuming.
- Deposits: use the business's own terms. Residential building deposits are capped in several states; check the state rules before stating a deposit.

## State requirements checklist

Residential building and trade work is regulated by each state and territory. Before the quote goes out, check with the user:

- [ ] The licence number appears on the quote if the state requires it.
- [ ] Above the state's threshold for a written contract, the quote is followed by a compliant contract, not used as the contract.
- [ ] Home warranty or domestic building insurance is arranged where the job value and work type require it.
- [ ] The deposit does not exceed the state cap for residential work.
- [ ] Any cooling-off rights for residential contracts are explained in the contract.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
