# Incidents on site: what to flag and how

The skill records and escalates. It never decides whether an incident is notifiable, never contacts the regulator, and never advises anyone to disturb an incident site. The user's WHS procedure and their responsible person decide.

## Which law

- New South Wales, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania, the ACT, the Northern Territory and Commonwealth workplaces: the model WHS Act as adopted in that jurisdiction.
- Victoria: the Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004, with similar notification and site-preservation duties.

## Flag as possibly notifiable (alert the user immediately)

Under the model WHS laws, a notifiable incident is a death, a serious injury or illness, or a dangerous incident arising out of the conduct of the business.

**Serious injury or illness** includes one requiring:

- immediate treatment as an in-patient in a hospital; or
- immediate treatment for an amputation, serious head injury, serious eye injury, serious burn, separation of skin from underlying tissue (degloving or scalping), spinal injury, loss of a bodily function, or serious lacerations; or
- medical treatment within 48 hours of exposure to a substance.

**Dangerous incident** means serious risk to a person from immediate or imminent exposure to, for example:

- an uncontrolled escape, spillage or leakage of a substance;
- an uncontrolled implosion, explosion or fire;
- an uncontrolled escape of gas, steam or a pressurised substance;
- electric shock;
- the fall or release from a height of any plant, substance or thing;
- the collapse, overturning, failure or malfunction of, or damage to, plant that requires authorisation (such as certain cranes);
- the collapse or partial collapse of a structure;
- the collapse or failure of an excavation or its shoring.

When in doubt, flag it. A false alarm costs a phone call; a missed notification is a breach.

## What the duty involves (for the user's process)

- [ ] Notify the regulator immediately after becoming aware, by the fastest available means; a written notice may be required within 48 hours.
- [ ] Preserve the site until an inspector arrives or directs otherwise. Exceptions include helping an injured person, removing a deceased person, making the site safe, or a police investigation.
- [ ] Keep a record of each notifiable incident (the model WHS laws require at least 5 years).

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.

## Alert message to the user (internal)

> POSSIBLE NOTIFIABLE INCIDENT at {project}, {location_on_site}, {time}.
> What happened (as reported): {facts_only}.
> Injured: {role_or_initials}, {injury_as_reported}, {treatment_status}.
> Source: {who_reported_and_how}.
> Your WHS process says {responsible_person} decides on notification and site preservation. I have not contacted anyone.

## How to record incidents in the daily report

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Time and location | As precise as the report allows |
| What happened | Observed facts, in the reporter's words where possible |
| People involved | Role or initials; full details only in the WHS incident record |
| Injury and treatment | As reported, minimum detail; no diagnosis |
| Immediate actions | First aid, area isolated, work stopped, who was told |
| Escalated to | Name of the responsible person and the time |
| Notifiable? | "Flagged as possibly notifiable; decision by {person}" or "Not flagged" |

Near misses and hazards are recorded too, with the control put in place. They are the most useful entries for preventing the next incident.
