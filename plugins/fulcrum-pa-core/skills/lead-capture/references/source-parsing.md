# Extracting leads from each source

## Email

- Use the From name and address, then the signature block for role, company, phone and website. Signatures are more reliable than the body.
- A forwarded email: the lead is the original sender, not the person who forwarded it. Record the forwarder as the referrer if relevant.
- Ignore quoted older threads except to check whether this is a repeat enquiry.
- Treat links and attachments with care: do not open unexpected attachments; note them.

## Web form

- Map fields by label, not by position. Keep the form name, page URL and any tracking parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `gclid`) as source detail.
- Record the exact consent wording beside the checkbox and whether it was ticked.
- Free-text "message" fields often hold the real need, timeframe and budget; read them fully.
- Junk patterns: links only, random characters in name fields, the same message across many forms.

## Call summary or call transcript

- The caller's number from the call log beats a number read out on the call.
- Capture: name, reason for calling, what they asked for, timeframe, budget if given, what was promised and by when.
- Separate what the caller said from what our side promised. A promise in the call is a task with a due date.
- If the summary came from an AI agent, check the transcript for any number, date or price before saving it.

## Voicemail

- Transcriptions mangle names and numbers. Mark the name and number "from voicemail, confirm" unless the number matches the caller ID.
- Capture the callback request and any preferred time; that drives the task due time.
- If the voicemail is unclear, the task is "Call back to clarify", not a guessed need.

## Business-card photo

- Read: name, title, company, mobile, landline, email, website, address, ABN if printed.
- Check each character that could be misread (0 and O, 1 and l, 5 and S). Run the phone and ABN through the normaliser; a checksum failure usually means a misread digit.
- Record where and when the card was received (event, meeting) as the source detail; ask the owner if unknown.
- Cards with several people or a handwritten note: capture only the person who gave the card and the note's content as context.

## Chat (website chat, WhatsApp, social messages)

- Use the profile name only as a starting point; ask for the full name in the reply if needed.
- The chat platform's phone number (for example WhatsApp) is usable as the mobile, in E.164.
- Record the platform and conversation link as the source.
- Short chats often lack contact details. Create the contact only when you have at least one way to reach the person outside the chat, or the platform keeps the thread open.

## Missing details

| Missing | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Surname | Store first name only; ask in the reply only if the next step needs it. |
| Email and phone both | Do not create a CRM contact; reply on the original channel and ask. |
| Company | Leave blank. Do not use the email domain unless it is clearly a business domain and the person did not say otherwise. |
| Need | Task "Qualify", no deal. |
