# Scope, inclusions, exclusions, assumptions and terms

## Writing the scope

- One line per deliverable, each with a quantity and a measurable end state: "Supply and install 4 pendant lights in the kitchen, tested and working", not "lighting work".
- Name the location, access arrangements and timing assumptions.
- Use the customer's words for the problem, and the business's words for the solution.
- If the customer asked for something the business will not do, say so in exclusions rather than leaving it silent.

## Exclusions to consider

Write down any exclusion a customer could reasonably assume is included.

| Work type | Common exclusions |
| --- | --- |
| Trades and installation | Rubbish removal, making good (patching, painting), permits and council fees, after-hours or weekend work, work outside the stated area, hidden defects, asbestos or hazardous material handling, items supplied by the customer |
| Professional services | Out-of-scope meetings, travel beyond a stated radius, third-party fees, rework after sign-off, additional revision rounds beyond the stated number |
| Software and web | Content writing, stock images and fonts, third-party subscriptions, hosting after launch, support after the warranty period, integrations not listed |
| Events and hospitality | Venue hire, extra hours, damage, cleaning beyond normal, dietary changes after the cut-off date |

## Assumptions

State each assumption that the price depends on, and what happens if it proves wrong:

- "Assumes clear access to the work area between 7am and 5pm on weekdays."
- "Assumes existing wiring is compliant; rectification work will be quoted as a variation."
- "Assumes two rounds of revisions; further rounds are charged at the stated hourly rate."

## Terms checklist

- [ ] Validity period and the date it ends.
- [ ] Deposit amount and when it is due; when work is booked (after deposit, or after acceptance).
- [ ] Payment terms for the balance and any progress payments.
- [ ] How variations are requested, priced and approved (in writing).
- [ ] Cancellation and rescheduling.
- [ ] Warranty the business offers, worded so it does not limit the consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law (which cannot be excluded).
- [ ] Insurance or licence numbers if the trade requires them to be shown.
- [ ] Ownership of materials or intellectual property until paid in full, if the user's terms say so.
- [ ] How to accept: signature, written reply, or deposit payment.

Flag for the user, and suggest they check with their adviser, any clause that is one-sided (for example, the business can change the price at will, or the customer cannot cancel in any circumstances). Unfair contract terms laws apply to standard-form contracts with consumers and small businesses.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.

## Follow-up schedule

| When | Action | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 2 | Confirm it arrived and is readable | Email; call for larger quotes |
| Day 5 to 7 | Offer to answer questions or walk through the options | Call or email |
| 3 business days before expiry | Remind of the validity date; note any price changes coming | Email |
| Expiry day | Close-out: ask whether to re-quote, update or close | Email |
| After a decline | Ask one question about why (price, timing, scope, chose someone else) and record it | Email or call |

Stop the sequence as soon as the customer accepts, declines or asks for no further contact.
