# Interview question bank

Ask in rounds of no more than five questions. Prefer stories about real recent cases to descriptions of the ideal process. After each round, play back what you heard in two or three lines before moving on.

## Round 1: scope and outcome

- What do you call this job, and how often does it come up?
- What starts it? (An email arrives, a date passes, a form is submitted, a customer calls.)
- What does "done" look like? What exists at the end that did not exist before?
- Who does it today, and who else touches it?
- What goes wrong when it is done badly or late? What does that cost?

## Round 2: walk through the last real case

- Tell me about the last time you did this, from the moment it started.
- What did you open or look at first? Where did each piece of information come from?
- What did you write, send or change, and in which system?
- How long did each part take, and which part is the most tedious?
- Is that case typical? What would a harder one look like?

## Round 3: decisions and rules

- Where in the process do you make a choice? What are the options?
- What makes you choose one option over another? Can you put a number on it? ("Over how many days?" "Above what amount?")
- What makes you stop and think, or ask someone else?
- What are the exceptions you see every month? Every year?
- What do you never do, even if a customer asks?

Turn every vague answer into a rule by asking for the threshold:

| They say | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| "If it's urgent" | "What makes something urgent? A date, an amount, a person?" |
| "A while" | "How many days before you act?" |
| "Big jobs" | "Above what value or size?" |
| "Good customers" | "How do you tell? Years with you, spend, paying on time?" |
| "I just know" | "Tell me about the last time you knew. What did you notice?" |

## Round 4: actions, approvals and checks

- Which steps send something to a customer, supplier or staff member? On which channel and from which account?
- Which of those would you want to see before they go? (Default: all of them.)
- Are there rules you must follow: consent, unsubscribe, calling hours, recording, privacy, NDIS, industry codes?
- How do you check that it worked? Where would you look?
- If something fails halfway (an email bounces, a booking errors), what do you do?

## Round 5: boundaries and examples

- What similar jobs is this not? Where does it hand over to someone else?
- What would you never want the assistant to do in this process?
- Can you share one real example (we will replace all names and figures)?
- Who should review the finished skill?

## SOP gap check

Read the SOP once fully, then look for these gaps. Ask only about what is missing.

| Gap | Signals in the SOP | Question to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No trigger | Starts with "Step 1: open..." | "What tells you it is time to start?" |
| Vague threshold | "promptly", "large", "if needed", "as appropriate" | "What number would you put on that?" |
| Missing owner | Passive voice: "the invoice is sent" | "Who does that step?" |
| Hidden approval | "Once agreed", "after sign-off" | "Who agrees, and how do they say yes?" |
| No exception path | Only the happy path | "What happens when <step> fails or data is missing?" |
| No definition of done | Ends with an action, not a result | "How do you know it is finished and correct?" |
| Stale facts | Prices, names, old system screens | "Where is the current source for this?" |
| Tool-specific clicks | "Click the blue button" | "What is the step trying to achieve?" Write the intent; mention the tool only in "With Fulcrum". |

## Interview etiquette

- Use the owner's words for steps and names; they will recognise their process.
- Do not suggest improvements during the interview. Note them and offer them after the teach-back.
- Stop when answers repeat. A good skill needs the rules and exceptions, not every detail.
