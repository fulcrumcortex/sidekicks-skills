# Requesting reviews ethically: checklist

Based on the ACCC's guidance on online reviews for businesses and on common review-platform rules. Where a platform's own rules are stricter, follow the platform.

## Who you ask

- [ ] Ask every customer after a completed service, or a consistent sample (for example every customer in a week). Do not choose only customers you expect to be happy.
- [ ] **No review gating.** Do not ask "Were you happy?" first and then send only the happy ones to a public review site while routing unhappy ones to private feedback.
- [ ] Do not ask staff, owners, family, friends or suppliers to review the business, and do not have anyone post reviews of competitors.
- [ ] Do not buy reviews, use review exchanges, or pay anyone to write them.

## Incentives

- [ ] Do not offer anything (discounts, entries in a draw, gifts, credit) in return for a positive review.
- [ ] Many platforms, including Google, do not allow any incentive for a review at all, positive or not. Check the platform's current policy before offering anything.
- [ ] Where a platform does allow an incentive, it must not depend on the review being positive, and the reviewer and readers should be told about it.
- [ ] Never make a refund, a fix or a resolution conditional on removing or changing a review.

## What you publish

- [ ] Do not remove, hide or hold back genuine negative reviews on your own website or social pages. Moderate only by consistent rules (abuse, private information, spam) applied to positive and negative reviews alike.
- [ ] Do not edit a review in a way that changes its meaning, or present selected testimonials as typical if they are not.
- [ ] Disclose any commercial relationship with a reviewer (for example a paid partner or an affiliate).
- [ ] Registered health practitioners: Ahpra's advertising guidelines restrict testimonials about clinical aspects of care. Check the current guidelines before asking patients for reviews to use in advertising or reposting any review.

## How you ask

- [ ] Ask after the service is complete and the customer has had a fair chance to judge it.
- [ ] One request and at most one reminder.
- [ ] Email or SMS requests are treated as commercial messages: send only where there is consent (a recent purchase usually supports inferred consent), identify the business, and include a working unsubscribe.
- [ ] Do not pressure people in person or on the phone, and do not ask while they are paying or in a vulnerable moment.
- [ ] Disability support participants and other vulnerable customers: make it clear that leaving a review is optional and has no effect on their services, and offer accessible ways to give feedback.
- [ ] Give a link to the review page; do not suggest wording or a star rating.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
