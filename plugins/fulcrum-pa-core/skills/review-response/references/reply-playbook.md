# Review reply playbook

## Classify

| Class | Signals | Reply? | Reply within | Escalate? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Praise | 4 or 5 stars, positive text | Yes | 7 days | No |
| Mixed | 3 stars, or praise plus a criticism | Yes | 3 business days | Only if the criticism is serious |
| Complaint | 1 or 2 stars, a specific failure (service, quality, billing, staff, wait time) | Yes, take offline | 1 to 2 business days | Owner or manager informed |
| Fake or suspicious | No matching customer, wrong business or location, services you do not offer, a cluster of similar reviews in a short time, a competitor's name, off-topic text | Report to the platform; a neutral reply only if needed | Report within 2 business days | Owner informed |
| Contains private information | The reviewer's or someone else's health, disability, contact details, account details, or a staff member's personal details | Reply generically, never repeat it; report if it exposes someone else | 1 to 2 business days | Owner informed |
| Serious allegation | Harm, abuse, neglect, discrimination, safety, professional misconduct, a legal threat, or a reviewer who appears at risk | Holding reply only, after approval | Same day to the owner | Yes, before anything else |
| Abusive or threatening | Insults, threats, hate speech | Usually no reply; report to the platform | Same day | Yes if any threat of harm |

Ratings without text: thank praise briefly; for one-star ratings without text, a short invitation to get in touch is enough.

## Reply patterns

**Praise**

Thank them, mention one non-private detail they raised, and stop. No sales pitch, no request to come back "for our new service".

> Thanks so much, Priya. We're glad the new opening hours made it easier to fit a visit in. - Sam, front desk

**Mixed**

Thank them, acknowledge the problem in their words, say what has changed only if it really has, invite contact.

> Thanks for the feedback, Jordan. You're right that the wait on Saturday was too long, and we've added a second person to the counter on weekends since then. If you'd like to tell us more, email feedback@example.com. - Alex, manager

**Complaint**

Acknowledge, apologise for the experience (not for facts you have not checked), give a named private route, say who follows up. Do not argue, explain at length, or offer compensation in public.

> Hi Chris, I'm sorry this wasn't the experience you expected. I'd like to understand what happened and put it right where we can. Please call me on 0400 000 000 or email manager@example.com, and I'll get back to you within one business day. - Alex, manager

**Fake or suspicious (neutral)**

Report it first. If a reply is needed, do not accuse anyone of lying.

> Thanks for taking the time to post. We take all feedback seriously and would like to look into this. Please contact us on 0400 000 000 so we can follow it up. - Alex, manager

For a general business you may say "We can't find a booking under this name"; for a confidential service, never say that (see below).

**Contains private information**

Do not repeat, quote or confirm any of it.

> Thank you for your feedback. To protect everyone's privacy we don't discuss individual circumstances here, but we'd like to talk with you directly. Please call 0400 000 000 and ask for Alex.

**Serious allegation (holding reply, owner-approved only)**

> We take what you've described very seriously. We'd like to speak with you directly, and our manager will be in touch, or you can call 0400 000 000.

## Confidential services: clinics, allied health, disability support, legal, financial

Health and disability information is sensitive information under the Privacy Act. A public reply can disclose it even when the reviewer raised it first.

- [ ] Do not confirm or deny that the reviewer is, or was, a patient, client or participant.
- [ ] Do not mention appointments, treatments, diagnoses, medications, results, NDIS plans or funding, support workers' names, dates or locations of visits.
- [ ] Do not say "we have no record of you"; it reveals information about who is a client.
- [ ] Do not correct the reviewer's account of their care in public, even if it is wrong.
- [ ] Use: "We can't discuss anyone's care publicly, but we'd like to talk with you. Please call [number]."
- [ ] Contact the person privately only through the channel they already use with the business, and record it.
- [ ] Allegations about care quality, safety, abuse or neglect go to the practice principal or the provider's incident process the same day. For NDIS providers, check whether the matter is a reportable incident.
- [ ] Do not reuse reviews in advertising without checking current Ahpra guidance on testimonials (registered health practitioners).

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.

## Style rules

- Use the reviewer's displayed first name, or no name.
- Sign with a first name and role. No staff surnames.
- One to three sentences for praise; three to five for mixed and complaints.
- Australian English, plain words, no exclamation-mark strings, no emojis, no keyword stuffing.
- Vary wording across a batch; identical replies read as automated.
- Never promise something the business has not agreed to do.
- Do not reply defensively to old reviews; a calm, short reply is fine at any age.
