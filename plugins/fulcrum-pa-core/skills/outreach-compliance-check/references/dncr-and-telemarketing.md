# Do Not Call Register and telemarketing checklist: call campaigns

Applies to telemarketing calls: calls that offer, advertise or promote goods or services, or seek donations. Includes calls made by AI voice agents and pre-recorded messages.

## 1. Do Not Call Register

- [ ] **The user washed the list** against the Register, through their own Register account or through an access seeker. No Fulcrum product washes numbers against the Register; Lyre's do-not-contact list is the organisation's own list, which is a different control. (Block if missing.)
- [ ] **The wash is no more than 30 days old on every call date**, including retry waves. Wash results are valid for 30 days. (Block if older.)
- [ ] Registered numbers are removed unless the person's consent to the call is recorded (who, when, how, scope).
- [ ] Exemptions are applied narrowly. They cover certain callers only, such as some government bodies, registered charities, political parties and candidates, religious organisations, and educational institutions calling current or former students' households. A business is not exempt because a call is "a courtesy call" or "only for existing customers". Exempt callers still follow the calling hours and identification rules.
- [ ] Mobile and business numbers were washed too.

## 2. Calling hours (Telemarketing and Research Calls Industry Standard)

- [ ] Calls only in the **recipient's local time**: Monday to Friday 9am to 8pm, Saturday 9am to 5pm, unless the person agreed to another time.
- [ ] No calls on Sunday or on a national public holiday (1 January, 26 January, Good Friday, Easter Monday, 25 April, 25 December, 26 December). Check the current standard for substitute days.
- [ ] A platform window set in one city's time is adjusted for recipients in other time zones. In daylight saving months, 9am in Sydney is 6am in Perth and 8am in Brisbane.
- [ ] Recipients with unknown location are called only in the window that is valid in every Australian time zone.

## 3. During the call

- [ ] The call starts with the caller's name, the business and the purpose.
- [ ] Caller ID is shown and the number can be called back.
- [ ] The call ends promptly when the person asks.
- [ ] The agent can say how the business obtained the number, if asked.

## 4. AI and recording disclosure

- [ ] An AI voice agent says at the start that the caller is an AI. It never says or implies it is a person; misleading conduct rules under the Australian Consumer Law apply to what it says.
- [ ] The call says it is recorded. State recording laws differ and some require all parties' consent; disclosure at the start of every call is the practical way to meet them.
- [ ] A person who objects to recording is offered a call from a person instead of continuing the recorded call.
- [ ] "Skip disclosure" settings for known callers are not used for campaign calls to people who have not already consented to recording.

## 5. Opt-outs

- [ ] A stop request ends the call, and the number goes on the organisation's do-not-contact list the same day.
- [ ] The person is removed from every other active campaign, journey and follow-up task.
- [ ] An SMS STOP reply also triggers a check of call lists.

## 6. Content

- [ ] The script makes no misleading claims (prices, savings, urgency, government affiliation, prior relationship).
- [ ] The script does not collect payment card details or sensitive information on an AI call.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
