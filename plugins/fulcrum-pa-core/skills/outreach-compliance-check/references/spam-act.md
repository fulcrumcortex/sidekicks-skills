# Spam Act checklist: commercial email, SMS and instant messages

The Spam Act 2003 covers commercial electronic messages (email, SMS, MMS and instant messages) with an Australian link. It does not cover voice calls; those fall under the Do Not Call Register and the telemarketing standard.

## 1. Is it a commercial electronic message?

- [ ] Any purpose of the message is to offer, advertise or promote goods, services, land, a business or investment opportunity, or the sender. If yes, the whole checklist applies.
- [ ] A transactional or factual message (receipt, delivery notice, appointment confirmation, invoice reminder) with no promotional content is usually not commercial. Adding an offer, a "you might also like" block or a promotional banner makes it commercial.
- [ ] Newsletters that promote the business, "checking in" messages that lead to an offer, and review requests are treated as commercial for this check.
- [ ] Possible exemption: some messages from government bodies, registered charities, political parties, religious organisations and educational institutions (to current or former students), and purely factual messages, are designated messages exempt from the consent and unsubscribe rules. They must still identify the sender. Confirm the sender really falls in the category.

## 2. Consent

| Status | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Express consent** | The person actively agreed: an unticked box they ticked, a form with clear wording, a recorded verbal yes. Record who, when, how and the exact wording. Pre-ticked boxes and consent buried in terms are weak evidence. |
| **Inferred consent: relationship** | An existing business or other relationship where the person would reasonably expect these messages, such as a current customer receiving offers related to what they bought. The older and thinner the relationship, the weaker the inference. |
| **Inferred consent: conspicuous publication** | A work address published publicly (for example on a company website), the message relates directly to the person's role or business, and the publication does not say they do not want unsolicited messages. A personal address found online does not qualify. |

- [ ] Every contact has a recorded consent basis. The sender must be able to prove consent if asked.
- [ ] Bought, rented or partner lists have evidence that consent covers messages from this sender. A seller's statement that the list is "opted in" is not evidence on its own.
- [ ] No message is sent to ask for consent from people who have not consented; that message is itself commercial.
- [ ] Consent for one channel or purpose is not stretched to another (email consent is not SMS consent).
- [ ] No contact has unsubscribed, bounced repeatedly or reported spam.

## 3. Sender identification

- [ ] The message clearly names the individual or organisation that authorised it.
- [ ] It includes accurate contact details for the sender that will remain valid for at least 30 days after sending.
- [ ] The "from" name, address or SMS sender ID is not misleading and matches the business.
- [ ] For SMS sent from a name rather than a number: Australia has introduced an SMS sender ID register run by the ACMA. Confirm with the SMS provider that the sender name meets current requirements.

## 4. Unsubscribe

- [ ] Every commercial message has a clear, conspicuous way to unsubscribe.
- [ ] It works for at least 30 days after the message is sent.
- [ ] It costs nothing beyond a standard message (a reply of STOP at the normal rate is acceptable) and needs no login, password, fee or extra personal information.
- [ ] If SMS is sent from a sender ID that cannot receive replies, another opt-out route is in the message (a short link or a number).
- [ ] Requests are honoured within five working days, across every tool that sends to that person.
- [ ] Test it: unsubscribe a test address or number and confirm it is suppressed.

## 5. Addresses and content

- [ ] No address-harvesting software was used, and no harvested list is in the audience.
- [ ] The subject line and preview text are not misleading.
- [ ] Claims, prices and offers are accurate and not misleading under the Australian Consumer Law: no false urgency, invented prior relationship ("following up your enquiry" when there was none), or prize claims.
- [ ] Links go to the business's own pages or known services.

## 6. Timing and volume (good practice)

- [ ] SMS is not sent late at night or early morning; use 9am to 8pm in the recipient's time zone as a default.
- [ ] Frequency is reasonable for the consent given; a spike in unsubscribes or complaints is a signal to pause.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
