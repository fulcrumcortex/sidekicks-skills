# Research rules for a meeting brief

## Internal sources, in order of trust

1. **CRM records**: contact, company, deals, tasks, tickets, invoices. Most reliable for dates, amounts and stages.
2. **Email threads** with the attendees or their domain, last 90 days (12 months for a renewal). Reliable for what was said and promised.
3. **Previous meeting notes and transcripts**, if they exist and the owner can access them.
4. **Colleagues' notes** on the timeline. Attribute them: "per Sam's note, 14 Oct".

When sources disagree, the most recent written record from the customer wins over an internal note; flag the conflict in the brief.

## What to extract

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Relationship start | First recorded interaction and how they found us (source). |
| Last three meaningful interactions | Calls, meetings or emails with substance. Skip auto-replies and newsletters. |
| Promises | Anything either side said they would do, with date and whether it happened. Broken promises by our side go first. |
| Open deals | Name, stage, amount, expected close date, days since last activity, next step. |
| Open issues | Complaints, support conversations, defects, late deliveries. Status and age. |
| Money | Unpaid invoices with age; recent payments; credit notes. |
| People | For each attendee: role, how long we have dealt with them, their stated priorities, preferred channel. |
| Decision process | Who decides, who signs, budget timing, procurement steps, if recorded. |

## Attendee matching

- Match guests to CRM contacts by email address first. Name-only matches are "possible", not confirmed.
- A guest at a generic address (info@, admin@, bookings@) is "unknown person at <company>".
- A guest not in the CRM: list as "Not in CRM". Do not create the record while preparing; suggest `lead-capture` afterwards if they are new.

## Public information limits

Use public research only when the owner has allowed it for this meeting or for meetings of this type.

Allowed, business only:

- The organisation's own website: what they do, locations, team size if stated, recent announcements.
- Recent news about the organisation from reputable outlets (last 12 months).
- The attendee's current role and tenure from a public professional profile or the company site.
- Public registers for business identity (for example, the business name and ABN status).

Not allowed, even if public:

- Personal social media, family members, home address, photos of the person outside work.
- Health, religion, political views, union membership, sexual orientation, criminal record or other sensitive information.
- Paid people-search or data-broker sites.
- Anything behind a login the owner has not authorised for this purpose.

Record the URL and date read for every public fact. If a public claim matters to the meeting (for example, "expanding to Perth"), mark it "public, unverified" and turn it into a question.

## When data is thin

- No CRM history and no mail: write a short "First contact" brief: what we know from the booking or enquiry, three discovery questions, and the next step. Do not pad with generic industry text.
- Unknown goal: propose one from the deal stage:

| Stage | Default goal | Default next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead or new enquiry | Confirm need, timing, budget range and decision maker | Agreed discovery call or site visit |
| Qualified | Agree scope and success criteria | Date for proposal walk-through |
| Proposal sent | Surface concerns and gaps | Verbal yes, or a list of changes with a date |
| Negotiation | Close remaining terms | Signed agreement or purchase order by a date |
| Customer, check-in | Confirm satisfaction, find expansion or risk | Referral, case study, or a scoped add-on |
| Complaint | Understand impact and agree a fix | Written remedy with dates the customer accepts |
