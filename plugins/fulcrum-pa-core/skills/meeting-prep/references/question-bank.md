# Question bank and objection handling

Pick five to seven questions for the meeting type. Order them: context, then problem, then impact, then decision, then next step. Rewrite them in the owner's voice and with the customer's facts.

## Discovery (first meeting)

- What prompted you to look at this now?
- How are you handling it today, and what does that cost you in time or money?
- What would a good result look like in three months?
- Who else is involved in deciding, and who signs off?
- Is there a date that matters (a season, a launch, end of financial year)?
- Have you set a budget range, or would it help if I outlined typical ranges?
- What would stop this from going ahead?

## Proposal walk-through

- Before I go through it, has anything changed since we last spoke?
- Which part of the proposal matters most to you?
- Is anything missing or more than you need?
- How does this compare with what else you are considering?
- What would you need to see to approve it?
- What does your approval process look like from here, and how long does it usually take?

## Negotiation

- Which terms are fixed for you and which have room?
- If we can solve <their concern>, are you ready to proceed?
- Would a different scope, staging or payment schedule help more than a lower price?
- What date do you need to sign by to hit your start date?

## Renewal or check-in

- What has worked well, and what has been frustrating?
- What is changing in your business over the next six to twelve months?
- Who else in your team should we be talking to?
- Would you be comfortable referring us or giving a short testimonial?

## Complaint or escalation

- Can you walk me through what happened, from your side?
- What has it cost you, in time, money or with your own customers?
- What would a fair fix look like to you?
- How would you like us to keep you updated, and how often?

Never argue facts in the first ten minutes. Acknowledge, confirm the impact, then agree the remedy and dates.

## Supplier meeting

- What lead times and price changes should we plan for over the next quarter?
- What would make us a better customer for you (forecasts, payment timing, order size)?
- Who is our contact when something goes wrong, and how fast do you respond?

## Recruitment

Use the role's selection criteria. Avoid questions about age, family plans, health, religion or other protected attributes.

## Likely objections

Use only objections that the record suggests. Each needs evidence from the record and a response the owner can adapt.

| Objection | Evidence to look for | Response pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "It's too expensive." | Budget comment, competitor quote, discount request | Go back to the cost of the problem; offer scope or staging options before price changes. Never discount in the meeting without the owner's limit. |
| "Not the right time." | Delays, close date moved, "after EOFY" | Ask what changes at the later date; agree a specific re-contact date and what will be ready then. |
| "We need to talk to <someone>." | New name in the thread, procurement mention | Offer to brief that person directly; ask what they will care about; book the follow-up before leaving. |
| "We're happy with our current provider." | Mention of incumbent | Ask what they would improve; offer a small, low-risk first step. |
| "Your last delivery was late." | Open complaint, late job | Acknowledge first, state what changed, offer a concrete safeguard. |
| "Can you do it faster?" | Deadline in thread | Confirm the real deadline; show what is possible and what it trades off. |
| "Send me something in writing." | Repeated requests for info, no engagement | Agree, then ask which two questions the document must answer and book a time to discuss it. |

## Desired next step

Write one primary and one fallback. Both must be specific, have a date, and be something the other side commits to.

- Primary: "Signed acceptance by Fri 20 Nov."
- Fallback: "Call with their finance lead booked before Wed 18 Nov."
