# Escalation ladder, tone and pre-chase checks

## The ladder

Days are days past the due date of the oldest unpaid invoice. Move up at most one stage per contact, and leave at least five business days between contacts unless the debtor promised a date.

| Stage | Days overdue | Channel | Purpose | Tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Not yet due | Email (optional) | Courtesy reminder 3 days before due, for new customers or large invoices | Helpful |
| 1 Friendly nudge | 1 to 7 | Email, invoice attached | Assume oversight; make paying easy | Warm, brief |
| 2 Firm reminder | 8 to 14 | Email, SMS to the billing contact if consent and number are confirmed | Ask for a payment date | Clear, direct |
| 3 Phone call | 15 to 30 | Call by a person (or an AI agent only for a simple reminder, with disclosure) | Find out why, agree a date or plan | Calm, problem-solving |
| 4 Statement and call | 31 to 45 | Statement of account by email, follow-up call | Show the full position; confirm the agreed date in writing | Businesslike |
| 5 Final notice and payment plan | 46 to 60 | Email and letter, final notice with a deadline of 7 days; offer a payment plan | Last in-house step; state the real next step | Formal, factual |
| 6 External options | 61+ and the final notice deadline has passed | Decision by the user | Choose recovery, plan or write-off | Not sent until decided |

A payment plan can be offered at any stage the moment the debtor engages and asks for time. Do not wait for stage 5.

## Tone by relationship

| Relationship | Adjustment |
| --- | --- |
| Long-standing, normally pays on time, first lapse | One stage softer. The owner or account manager may call personally at stage 2 instead of emailing. |
| New or standard customer | Ladder as written. |
| Repeat late payer (two of the last four invoices paid more than 14 days late) | One stage faster. Recommend the user pause new work or require a deposit from 31 days, if the terms allow. |
| Government agency or large corporate | Process-focused, sent to accounts payable: confirm invoice received, PO number, supplier set-up and the next payment run date. Escalate to the purchasing contact only at stage 4. |
| Individual consumer | Plain language. Watch for hardship signals and offer a plan early. The debt collection guideline applies in full. |
| Strategic account (the user names it) | The user approves every contact from stage 3. |

## Pre-chase checks

Run these for every debtor before drafting. Any "yes" puts the debt on hold.

1. **Dispute or complaint.** Is there an open complaint, a query about the work or the amount, a credit note request, a warranty claim or unfinished work? Hold the disputed invoice. Chase an undisputed invoice for the same debtor only if the user approves.
2. **Paid but not reconciled.** Search the bank feed and payment provider for the amount (and part amounts) over the last five business days, and the inbox for the invoice number, debtor name and "remittance". If a likely payment exists, hold and ask the user to reconcile.
3. **Wrong or missing contact.** Is the reminder going to accounts payable or the person who approves payment, rather than a project contact? Did earlier emails bounce? Did the contact leave?
4. **Invoice not received or not valid.** Was the invoice actually delivered? Does it show the correct legal entity, ABN, PO number, GST and due date under the agreed terms? Many large organisations will not pay an invoice without a PO number.
5. **Credits and part payments.** Apply credit notes first. Treat a part payment as a sign of an arrangement or a short payment; ask before chasing the rest.
6. **Existing arrangement.** Is there an agreed payment plan or a promised date that has not passed? Do not chase instalments not yet due.
7. **Insolvency or death.** Search for administration, liquidation or bankruptcy notices, and check for a deceased estate. Stop and tell the user.
8. **Size against effort.** If the balance is smaller than the likely cost of chasing, suggest adding it to the next invoice or a write-off review rather than escalating.

## Contact rules (debt collection guideline)

The ACCC and ASIC debt collection guideline applies to businesses collecting their own debts as well as to collectors. Check the current edition. Practical points:

- [ ] Contact no more than three times a week or ten times a month in total across all channels, counting only contact that reaches the debtor.
- [ ] Phone between 7.30am and 9pm on weekdays and 9am to 9pm on weekends, in the debtor's time zone. No contact on national public holidays.
- [ ] Do not mislead about the debt, its amount, or the consequences of not paying. Do not imply legal action has started or will start unless the user has decided it will.
- [ ] Do not mention credit reporting unless the business is actually able to report to a credit reporting body.
- [ ] Do not disclose the debt to third parties. Use neutral voicemail and SMS wording where the number might be shared.
- [ ] Honour a request to communicate in writing only, or through a representative such as a financial counsellor or lawyer.
- [ ] Do not harass, embarrass or pressure. Harassment and coercion are prohibited under the Australian Consumer Law.
- [ ] Charge interest or fees only where the agreed terms allow them, and only reasonable amounts.
- [ ] A payment reminder with no promotional content is generally not a commercial message under the Spam Act, but it must still identify the business. Keep offers out of reminders.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.

## External options (stage 6)

Present these to the user as choices, with the amount, age and relationship. The user decides; the skill does not start any of them.

| Option | Typically suits | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal payment plan | Debtor engaged but short of cash | Written, with instalment dates and what happens if one is missed. |
| Debt collection agency | Unresponsive debtor, moderate amount | Usually commission-based. Check the agency is licensed where the state requires it. |
| Lawyer's letter of demand | Larger amount, debtor disputes without detail | Often prompts payment without proceedings. |
| Small claims in the state or territory tribunal or local court | Amount within the small claims limit | Limits and fees differ by state. |
| Mediation | Business-to-business dispute about the work | The Australian Small Business and Family Enterprise Ombudsman and state small business commissioners offer dispute support. |
| Security of payment claim | Construction work | Each state has its own construction payment legislation with strict time limits. Get advice early. |
| Creditor's statutory demand | Company debtor, undisputed debt above the statutory minimum | Serious step with formal requirements; needs a lawyer. |
| Write-off | Small balance or no prospect of recovery | Ask the accountant: a GST adjustment may be available for bad debts written off or overdue 12 months or more. |
